GUIDE 01

How home solar works

Solar photovoltaic (PV) panels convert sunlight into direct-current electricity. An inverter makes that electricity usable for most home circuits. Your home can use the energy as it's produced, while any surplus may be exported to the grid or stored in a compatible battery, depending on your system.

What affects production?

Your local solar resource, panel capacity, shading, roof angle, roof direction, temperature, wiring, and inverter performance all matter. A 6 kW array in a shaded location may produce much less than an equally sized system in full sun.

What does “kW” versus “kWh” mean?

kW is a measure of power or system capacity. kWh is the amount of electricity produced or consumed over time. A 1 kW system producing at its rated power for one hour generates 1 kWh, before losses.

A solar array typically does not supply household power during a grid outage unless the system includes approved backup equipment. Confirm that functionality before purchasing.
GUIDE 02

Will solar lower my bill?

Often it can, but savings depend on what you currently pay for electricity and how your utility compensates you for extra energy. Electricity used directly in your home generally offsets purchases at your applicable retail price. Energy exported to the grid may be credited at a different rate.

Four items to check on your bill

  1. Energy use: Look for the monthly kWh figure, ideally across an entire year.
  2. Energy rate: Know whether your rate changes by time of day or season.
  3. Fixed charges: Some costs remain even after installing solar.
  4. Export policy: Net metering and export credits differ by utility and state.

Understand the price you're comparing

Compare cash price, loan-financed price, any dealer fees, interest, equipment warranties, roof work, and projected maintenance. Savings models should spell out their assumptions about future utility rates, output decline, taxes, and incentives.

Our calculator treats your bill divided by a user-entered rate as an approximation of monthly energy use. That simplification is less reliable when your bill includes high fixed charges or complicated tariffs.
GUIDE 03

Do I need a solar battery?

A battery can shift when you use solar electricity and may support some household circuits during a grid outage. It is not required for every grid-tied solar installation.

Battery questions to ask

  • What is the battery's usable capacity in kWh?
  • What is its continuous and peak output in kW, and which loads can it handle?
  • What circuits will remain powered in a blackout?
  • Can the battery recharge from solar during an outage, and under what conditions?
  • What are the warranty, replacement, and installation costs?

Use our battery backup calculator to see how capacity and load interact. It does not model starting surges, inverter limits, recharging, or temperature.

GUIDE 04

How to compare solar installers

Obtain multiple written proposals with similar scope and assumptions. Ask each installer for annual production estimates, full equipment specifications, warranty coverage, roof and electrical work, permit responsibilities, and projected utility savings.

Compare more than the monthly payment

Some offers can have different cash prices, finance charges, escalators, prepayment terms, or equipment ownership arrangements. Ask what happens if you move, sell your home, refinance, or need roof repairs.

GUIDE 05 · UPDATED NOVEMBER 2026

Current solar incentives

The federal Residential Clean Energy Credit formerly available to qualifying homeowners for 30% of eligible costs is not available for expenditures after December 31, 2025. Tax rules can be complex, including timing and carryforwards, so confirm your situation with a qualified professional.

Other programs may still be available at the state, municipal, or utility level. Search by state or ZIP code using DSIRE, then verify the requirements directly with the administering agency.

Never accept a proposal that promises a particular tax credit without current documentation and personalized eligibility review.
